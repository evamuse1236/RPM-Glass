# Design: weekly review

Part of [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md). 

1. **How did last week's Results go?** Results whose deadline is still ahead are not judged. While the deck has cards, one quiet list follows the buttons: "Not due yet" (ring, then one facts line: "2 of 7 done", then the deadline; the Must time waits for step 4). The cards still to judge are counted by the tally, not listed again; once the deck is done the running Results join the carry list. The rest are a deck of cards, one at a time (prototype round4 r1): the Area (in the Area hue, when the Result has one), the title, its Purpose, then one meta row: a small ring and "4 of 5 done" (read aloud as "4 of 5 tasks done") with the deadline chip (at 5 of 5 the ring becomes a filled disc with a check), on a surface-container-high card sized to its content at every width; no cards peek below it. The card carries no count of its own: the tally ("0 of 2 decided") is the one counter line. The card follows the finger once a drag locks to the x axis (8px, as task rows do) and shows an "Achieved" or "Not yet" stamp; let go past 90px and it flies off and saves (right is Achieved, left is Not yet), otherwise it springs back. Not yet, Partly and Achieved under the deck do the same, and a tally shows how many are decided and the count of each verdict that is not zero. The three buttons are 40dp tonal pills. Every verdict has the snackbar's Undo, which brings the card back. When every card is decided, the green Wins card is the one celebration: each Achieved Result with an optional "What made it work?" field (saved as the Result's evidence). Then "Your verdicts" lists each other Result as one row (verdict icon, title, "Partly · 1 of 3 done"); a tap opens a menu of Not yet, Partly, Achieved to change it. Then one list, "Carry into this week": the open tasks of each Partly or Not yet Result under that Result's title as a quiet label-medium head on its own short row, a divider under it (on one column the first head is left out when it repeats the decided row just above it; a tap opens Carry, Defer or Drop; a redo button carries it; a facts line shows only once a task is deferred or dropped), then a "Still running" head over every Result still running, with "Carry all" in its heading, which carries every one not yet carried as one change with one Undo. Drop archives the task and can be undone. A step heading has no explainer line under it; at most one meta line of facts. In a window at least 840px wide the step is two columns: the deck (or the Wins card and verdicts) on the left, Not due yet (or Carry into this week) on the right, its heading on the tally's line. On the website the snackbar docks in the footer row, left of Next. On the phone, while a snackbar shows, the body ends above it (snackbar height + 16dp), so no row sits half hidden behind it; while the summary's rows continue below, a small round chevron ("Show more below") sits on the fade at the body's end and scrolls to them.
2. **Empty your head.** An inline field ("Ideas, wants, to-dos") adds what is typed to the Inbox on Enter (saved in the user's words, with Undo) without leaving the step. Under it, after a quiet "Think about" lead, one pill-shaped prompt per Area ("Studies", with its Area dot; a second row of prompts starts under the first prompt, not under the lead): a tap puts that Area's question in the field ("Studies: anything due, stuck or wanted?") and changes nothing else, so it never reads as a filter. Capture is a mic button inside the field. Below, an "Inbox" heading with its count leads the newest four Inbox tasks, shown inline; "Show all", the list's last row (the heading already gives the count), lists the rest ("Show fewer" folds them back). Each shows its Must star (alone) or date where it has one; the task that names a calendar clash shows a "Clash" tag with the event_busy icon in the error colour (named "Calendar clash, decided in step 4"). Tapping a task opens the planner's task sheet (edit, Delete in its menu); swiping schedules (right) or deletes (left) as in every task list.
3. **Sort the Inbox.** "To sort" lists each Inbox task as one row that is one button, named Choose a Result for "<task>": its words and a chevron. Only when the words clearly point to one Result (a word shared with the Result's own title, well ahead of the next) does the same button add a second line under the words: the Block icon and the Result's title in quiet on-surface-variant text (wrapping, never cut); then the chevron gives way to one quiet 40dp `drive_file_move` icon button in primary, named Add "<task>" to "<Result>", that takes the offer in one tap (with Undo), while the row itself still opens the chooser. Errands show just their words and the chevron. A right swipe adds the task to the proposed Result, a left swipe keeps it in the Inbox (both with Undo). Once per review the first suggested row peeks 48px to the right and back, showing the swipe (not under reduced motion). A tap opens a bottom sheet titled "Choose a Result" with the task under it: New Result and Leave in Inbox first, then every Result, likeliest first (the proposed one with a primary icon), each with the stacks icon, its deadline and its Area ("Due Mon 10:30 AM · Studies"). New Result asks "What's the Result?" and "Why does it matter?" (both needed). Back and Next stay in the footer throughout. Tasks sorted during the review appear under "Sorted" beneath their Result, each with a button back to the Inbox; tasks left on purpose appear under "Staying in the Inbox", each with a button to sort it after all (remembered with the review's progress, no plan change). The task naming a calendar clash is not sorted here and is left out of the list and its count; step 4 decides it. "Ask Jev" sits in the To sort header when the assistant is set up; nothing moves until the user confirms.
4. **Pick this week's Results.** One meta line, "3 picked · aim for 3 to 5", leads; nothing blocks Finish. Each calendar clash before Sunday comes first, as one compact row (described below). Then the Results, as one tonal list ordered by deadline, picked or not, then those without one: a leading checkbox picks it (an empty circle; picked, a filled check_circle in primary and the title turns medium weight; no row tint), then the Result (body-large) and one regular-weight facts line as on the Blocks list ("Due Mon 10:30 AM · ★ 2 h 15 min", "Carried from last week"; a near Result with no Must says "No Must set"); tapping the text expands it: its Purpose, then its open tasks under "Tap the tasks that must happen" (each row toggles Must, only Musts show the star, each task's day on its own line) and Open Plan. Results with no open tasks wait behind the list's last row ("3 Results with no open tasks"; open, they follow the others and the row says "Show fewer"). A Result's deadline is its latest open, dated, one-time task; nothing else is assumed. Each calendar clash, once, is a decision on one surface-container row: an event_busy icon in the error colour, "DAD exam vs GWBC session", "Monday 9 November · keep one" under it, then one row of choices: a two-line chip per event (its title over its time, "9 – 10 AM", named Keep <event>) and a "Later" text button at the row's end, on the same line as the chips. Calendars are read-only, so the answer becomes a task on Today: the user's own task naming the clash keeps its words and gains "Decided in the weekly review: keep DAD exam, move or skip GWBC session." in its notes; otherwise a 15-minute task says it. A decided clash keeps its title, says "Keep DAD exam · a task for Monday" under it with an event_available icon, and offers Change; Later adds "left for later" and offers Decide. Last, under a "Your week" heading, one outlined summary (in a window at least 840px wide it sits in a right column beside the clashes and Results, and stays in view while the list scrolls): a day strip from today to Sunday of day buttons, each saying its marks in words (★ the Must time already on that day, "Due" on a Result's deadline day, a red dot and "Clash" on a calendar-clash day; error container when Must time doesn't fit), a one-line legend, and a tapped day shown below the strip (its free time, 8 AM–10 PM minus events, the deadlines on it, picked or not, its Musts and its clash). Then one summary line ("6 h 20 min of Must · fits before every deadline", or, while Musts of Results with a deadline have no day, "4 h of Must with no day yet" first, with the Must total under it). Tapping it opens one row per picked Result, in deadline order, with the same deadline words and Must time as its row, its free time until that deadline, and Fits, Tight (over half the free time) or Short by. Each row counts the Musts due before it too, and says so when that changes the verdict. Undated Musts of Results with a deadline get a suggested day (the freest day before the deadline, never on or after it) that stays in view; "Give them these days" writes them in one change with Undo. Free time is 8 AM–10 PM minus busy events from the read-only calendar, and the review says so wherever it counts it; without a readable calendar it says "Calendar not connected" and shows only Must time. Finishing saves the week's focus and returns to Today.

The review says Result throughout: a Block is a Result with its Purpose and tasks, and the review only names the Result. Every step can be skipped, leaving keeps progress, and every change goes through the same Undo path as the planner. Next and Finish are the one main button and never disabled; in step 1 Next is outlined (primary words, outline edge, so it never matches the tonal verdict buttons) while a card is still to judge, and filled once all are decided; step 4's Finish review follows the same rule until a Result is picked; the top bar holds only Close and the title, since the step bar shows the step; each step's own counts ("1 of 2 decided", "To sort 3", "3 picked · 1 clash to decide") guide instead of a footer caption. The footer has no fade; a hairline shows while content continues underneath.
