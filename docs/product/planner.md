# Product: planner

Part of [PRODUCT.md](../../PRODUCT.md). 

- The planner follows Material 3 as Google's own apps use it (see [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md)). Four persistent destinations move outward through **Today · Blocks · Projects · Life**. Settings, Search and the weekly review are pushed screens, not extra tabs.
- Today answers, in order: what to do now, what is coming up, and the shape of the day. The date strip runs forward from today for seven days and marks deadline days (with the number of Results due when more than one) and calendar-clash days; tapping a day lists its deadlines and clash. One line under the strip says where the week's plan stands and opens the weekly review: this week's Results (only Results the user marked achieved count as achieved), or "Plan your week"; from Friday to Sunday, while the coming week has no Results, it says that week isn't planned and names the user's own "Weekly review" task today when there is one. The lead card holds the screen's one filled button. When there is at least half an hour free before the next timed task or busy event, it offers that free time ("Free 8:00 – 10:00 AM · in 1 h") to the nearest deadline (overdue first, then due by the end of the day after tomorrow) whose unscheduled tasks fit, whether or not other work for it is planned: the Result, those tasks, and one tap that schedules them back to back into the gap, with Undo (the gap never takes the task that sets the Result's deadline). Otherwise it is **Do next**: the next task under its Result (title, then Purpose on its own line, then the deadline, its countdown and the tasks left). **Coming up** lists, from the data only and soonest first, every other Result due in the next three days and every calendar clash in that window, each once, with one deadline format ("Due tomorrow 11:59 PM · in 41 h") and the tasks left. A Result due by the end of the day after tomorrow with nothing planned between today and its deadline says "not scheduled yet" and offers Add to today (its first undated task, with Undo); an overdue Result says so; a clash shows each overlapping item with its time span, says which task can move and which one is a Result's deadline, and offers Resolve (choose which overlapping task to move, with Undo, or for events only add a task today to sort it out, since calendars are read-only). Without calendar permission clashes and busy time are simply not checked. The rest of the day is one time-ordered agenda: tasks, each naming its Result, and calendar events interleaved, with the free time between them stated; the Inbox row shows the Inbox count. A Result's deadline comes from the planner's data and is never invented. The Must star shows only on Musts, everywhere; Must is set in the task sheet (or, in the weekly review, by tapping the task's row), never by a bare star on a non-Must row (Dara, 2026-10-03). Add task and Search are buttons at the bottom right, above the navigation bar; the proportional Timeline is a second view of the same day, in More options.
- The weekly review is a four-step route reached from Today's week line and the overflow menu: how last week's Results went (Achieved, Partly or Not yet for Results whose deadline has passed or that have none; wins are noticed with an optional line on what made them work; Results still running toward a later deadline are not judged but offered to carry into this week, each with a checkbox and one "Carry both" for all; Carry, Defer or Drop for unfinished tasks), empty your head (type straight into the Inbox, prompted Area by Area if wanted, or open Capture; an Inbox task opens the task sheet to edit or delete it, and swipes like any task row: right to schedule, left to delete), sort the Inbox (each task gets its Result one at a time: a suggested Result in one tap when the task's words clearly match that Result's title, Leave in Inbox in one tap for an errand, or Choose from every Result, with its deadline and Area, or a new Result with its Purpose; the task that names a calendar clash is left to step 4), and pick 3 to 5 Results for this week, nearest deadline first whether picked or not. A Result's deadline in the review is its latest open, dated, one-time task. Step 4 lists the Results first, each with its deadline and Must time, an unpicked one due by Sunday saying so on its row; a row opens to its Purpose and its tasks, where tapping a task toggles Must. Each calendar clash before Sunday appears once as a decision (which event stays, or Later); calendars are never written, so the decision becomes a task on Today, reusing the user's own task that names the clash (its words kept, the decision added to its notes). Then the week at a glance: each day's Must time and, in words, its deadlines and clashes (a tapped day shows them, with its free time), and a check of each picked Result against its own deadline: its Must time, plus the Must time of Results due before it, against the free time from now until that deadline, counted as 8 AM–10 PM minus busy events from the read-only calendar (the review states that assumption), using the same deadline and Must time its row shows. It suggests a day for each undated Must of a Result with a deadline (the freest day before it); nothing is dated until the user taps to accept, with Undo. Without calendar access it says the calendar isn't connected and shows Must time only. Every step can be skipped (Next and Finish only guide, never block: step 4 counts the clashes still to decide and the due Results not picked), progress is kept (including tasks left in the Inbox and clash decisions), and every change can be undone.
- Only the user marks a Result achieved, from the review or Block detail, with an optional line of evidence. Completing every task does not mark it.
- Blocks present **Result · Purpose · Plan**. Summary cards stay simple; Block detail holds Purpose, ordered Plan tasks, Must, and editing.
- Detail pages (Block, Project, Goal, Area) edit in place, with no Edit form: the title, Purpose and Notes (shown once there are any, or from More → Add notes) are text that takes the caret where it is tapped (a small pencil beside the Purpose and Notes labels says so at rest), and saves on Enter or on leaving the field, with Undo; an emptied title is never saved; Escape or Android Back first puts the saved words back. The parent line is an anchored menu that opens the parent or moves the item in one tap (a Block's Area · Project line lists the Projects and No project; a Project's Goal chip; a Goal's Area chip), a Goal's stretch (whole year, quarter or month) and year are two such menus, and an Area's rating is a slider on its page that moves in whole points, saved when let go (an unrated Area shows its eleven points as faint ticks with a hollow handle at the start and says Not rated, so it reads as something to drag but never as a value). There is no Edit pencil; Delete stays in More. A new Block, Project, Goal or Area is created at once and opens on its page with its placeholder title ("New Result") selected, so typing names it and Enter moves on to its Purpose; creating it can be undone. Only a Goal drafted by Capture still opens a form, to review its original words.
- A Result has no separate deadline field (decided 2026-10-03). Its due time is derived: the latest open, dated, one-time task in its Block (`blockDue`), such as "Submit the PDF · Sun 9:00 PM". The planner and weekly review use it to show what is close.
- The Blocks list is one list in order of urgency: Results due within the week ahead (or overdue) first, soonest first, then this week's Results (marked "Picked for this week"), then the rest. Each card has one facts line: its due line, the open Must time (so tonight's load compares across Results) and tasks done; the countdown lives on Block detail. Cards in a row from the same Project sit under one Area and Project line instead of each repeating it; with a Project filter on there is no such line.
- Block detail puts the Purpose under the title, then one row for where the Result stands (the deadline with its countdown, tasks done, and the open Must time), then "Is this Result achieved?" with Mark achieved as its own row, apart from the tick count, so the verdict is never below the Plan. The deadline turns to the error colour, with the countdown in words, when it is overdue or when the open Musts need a quarter or more of the clock time left. Plan order is the user's priority and is never re-sorted on its own. While dated open tasks are out of time order, Sort by time offers one tap that puts them in time order in the places dated tasks held, leaving undated tasks where the user put them, with Undo. There is no Reorder mode: holding a Plan row still for about 350ms lifts it and dragging moves it while the other rows slide out of its way (Google Tasks); letting go settles it into its slot and saves the order with Undo, without rebuilding the Plan. Moving before the hold is a scroll and a short tap still opens the task. Change Plan order in a task's menu, and Alt or Ctrl with the arrow keys, stay the accessible paths. Tasks are added from the inline "Add or find a task" row under the Plan, which also finds Inbox tasks (tasks in no Block) as you type and moves the one you tap into this Block, with Undo (Enter turns the typed words into the new Plan row where they are, in the same frame the field empties; the field is never rebuilt and keeps its focus and caret, so words typed straight after Enter go to the next task, and only its place moves down); there is no FAB, so nothing covers a row's Must star. Marking or unmarking Must, like every Plan change, offers Undo. Once scrolled, the top bar keeps the title and the deadline line in view.
- Projects summarize linked Blocks without nested card stacks. Life contains Vision, Quarter, Month, and Values, with Areas, Goals, Projects, ratings, and a labelled Wheel of Life.
- Task rows swipe as in Things and Todoist (Today's spine, Block Plans, the Inbox and Search), built for a right thumb: the row follows the finger over a reveal that names the action. Swiping right is **Done**, with Undo. Swiping left is **Schedule** (in the Inbox, **Move** to a Block): letting go opens one-tap choices anchored to the row (Today and Tomorrow keeping the task's time, Tonight 7 PM until 7 PM, Tomorrow morning 9 AM, the weekend or next week, No date for a dated task, and Pick a date…, which opens the task sheet with its date choices unfolded); a choice saves at once with Undo, so a reschedule is a swipe and one tap. Delete is never a swipe: a long press opens Must, Move to Block and Delete (Delete goes to Trash with Undo; nothing asks first). The weekly review's Inbox still swipes right to schedule and left to delete. Archive is in the task's More menu, beside Schedule (the accessible path to the same choices) and Delete.
- The task sheet is the editor; there is no separate Edit task sheet. At rest it shows only the title, When, Must and the Block, so it reads at a glance. When is one line ("Today, 10:00 AM · 1 h 30 min", with a bell when an alert is set); tapping it unfolds a row each for the day, the time, the length and the alert (the alert only once there is a time). Each chip saves at once with Undo and the card stays open, so a day, a time and a length are three taps in one place; Pick opens the phone's own date or time picker, and a new day keeps the time already set. Must is a switch. Repeat, notes, why, who could help and the original words sit under More details, which opens by itself when the task already has any of them. The first tap into the title puts the caret at the end of its words; the title's Enter (the keyboard's Next) moves on to the details. A task opened from the Inbox has a back arrow: it, Android Back and Escape return to the Inbox (its row shows a soft highlight that fades; focus rings are drawn for keyboard navigation only), while X closes both. A calendar or Plan clash never blocks a save: it is said in words, in the error colour, under the date. A task with no Block shows the Block its words point to (as the weekly review suggests it) one tap away. Android Back closes an open choice before the sheet, and typing is saved when the sheet closes.
- Quick add's title wraps onto as many lines as the words need, so they are always all in view. It shows the likeliest choices as chips without opening anything (Today, Tomorrow, the weekend or next week; the suggested and recent Block; estimate, Must, repeat, details) and unfolds the rest in place. A day written in the title ("tomorrow", "fri 9am") is used as the date: its words are highlighted in the title and its chip is marked, once (no separate line repeats it); tapping the marked chip removes it, and the words stay as typed. Typing # and part of a Block's name offers the matching Blocks as chips under the title; choosing one puts the task there and takes the #word out of the title (otherwise the words stay). A #word that names exactly one Block (two letters or more, starting a word of its title) chooses it as typed: the #word is highlighted and the Block chip is marked (tapping it again removes it), and Add takes the #word out of the title, so "Email the TA tomorrow #quiz" is added for tomorrow in that Block with no other tap. Add adds and closes; the keyboard's Done adds and keeps the sheet open for the next task, with the day and Block kept. Chip rows never cut a chip off: Time and Repeat show only their icon until set, and a row that still does not fit wraps; the Block chips keep one line (a long name shortens, large text wraps) so a suggestion appearing never grows the sheet. Original capture appears when it differs from the task title.
- The vocabulary is binding in UI text: **Area, Goal, Project, Block, Result, Purpose, Plan, Task, Must, No block, Inbox**.
